## 5. Insights and Learnings

- **The "pit of success" frame is a missing piece of 2026 agentic-coding vocabulary.** The construct (Mariani, 2003) is present everywhere in current harness-engineering literature under five different names: harness engineering (OpenAI, Anthropic), constraint-driven success (Vercel tools-subtraction), grounding (hallucination-mitigation papers), path of least resistance (Surge HQ "693 lines"), and context engineering. Nobody ties them back to the original. Worth owning as conceptual territory — no competition for the combined phrase in search, and it cleanly separates harness *capability* from harness *gravity*.

- **A taxonomy of pit-creation techniques has three stable categories.** Point-of-decision friction (tax the shortcut immediately: hooks, required output shapes, inline annotations), context-loading defaults (remove the excuse for skipping: auto-inject patterns, preload AC), and intake-outcome shaping (quality at the boundaries: ticket gates, isolated reviewer, post-merge audit). Every working harness technique seems to fit one of these; no fourth category surfaced under pressure.

- **Agile Flow's strength is structure; its remaining gap is verification.** Tickets, branches, review gates, pre-push hooks — all structure. What's missing is verification that the agent actually did the thing the structure asked for. Grounding is suggested but not required. Evidence is claimed but not posted. Reviewer inherits worker framing. Pattern library is passive. Ten tickets now capture these gaps.

- **"Friction at the point of decision" is the load-bearing design principle, not "friction anywhere."** Delayed friction (reviewer catches it 3 days later) doesn't shape behavior — the decision is already made, the feedback doesn't reach the model that made the mistake. This is why hooks and inline skill-step requirements matter more than end-of-process gates. Agile Flow's remaining weakness is that most of its verification happens at PR-review time, not at decision time.

- **Three-question harness audit is portable.** (1) Is the cheapest next action the correct next action at each decision point? (2) Does relevant prior knowledge arrive in context before the agent decides whether to look? (3) When output is subtly wrong, does something fail visibly at the decision point? Applied to Agile Flow it surfaced exactly the gaps the 10 tickets address. Likely applicable to any agent harness.

- **Blog-post-as-synthesis works when the construct is both borrowed and novel.** Borrowing "pit of success" (old, credible, underused here) plus synthesizing the 2026 literature into a taxonomy (new) plus grounding in Agile Flow tickets (concrete evidence) produced a piece with clear positioning. Pure-synthesis posts without a borrowed frame tend to feel meandering; pure-novel-concept posts without synthesis feel unmoored.
